## How to compare crypto trading apps without reading ten reviews

Compare the all-in cost of one order you would actually place, then check the two things that are not on the pricing page: how you fund the account and what a withdrawal costs.

**Commission plus spread.** A quoted "1%" is rarely the whole bill. NerdWallet's $200 Bitcoin test on Coinbase showed a 1% spread plus a $3.68 fee, which works out to 2.84% all-in. On Uphold the same test produced a $0.99 stated fee and roughly $0.02 of spread, an effective 0.505%. On OKX, spot trades are charged a published maker/taker rate, but the Convert tool quotes a rate set by OKX rather than showing a fee, so a swap there can cost more than a limit order on the spot book.

**Funding method.** Every platform in this comparison charges less when you keep the card out of it. NerdWallet's advice is blunt: fund and trade by ACH bank connection rather than debit or credit card to reach the lowest rate. OKX's EU customers can deposit euros by SEPA transfer through OKX Europe, the MiCA-licensed Malta entity; card purchases carry the processor's cost.

**Withdrawal fee and limits.** This is the cost that compounds over years. NerdWallet cites rising Bitcoin withdrawal fees as the main reason it downgraded Crypto.com's app this year, and Money.com put the top withdrawal fee there at 0.0004 BTC, more than $40 at the time.

## Which app fits which trader

**Under $1,000 a month, buying and holding:** OKX at the standard tier or Kraken. Both keep commission near or under 0.1–0.25%, and OKX's free crypto deposits mean you're not paying to move coins in. Skip Gemini's retail tier unless you specifically want its custody and banking arrangements.

**Placing a handful of larger orders:** maker rates matter more than headline percentages. On OKX that means 0.08% instead of 0.10%, and posting the order rather than crossing the spread.

**Trading perpetuals:** OKX's 0.02% / 0.05% futures rate is competitive, but funding is the cost that decides whether a leveraged position is worth holding overnight. It's paid between traders, and on a long hold it can exceed the trading fee.

**Withdrawing to your own wallet regularly:** check the withdrawal network fee on the confirmation screen every time, and pick the cheapest network your wallet supports. Watch the 24-hour limit if you're moving a balance in several coins at once.

**Outside the regions OKX serves, or in New York or Maine:** Kraken and Gemini are ruled out in those states too, so start from what's actually available where you live before comparing rates.
